# Estuary Ink art direction

Crane Reach is an aged parchment field set in a greener night-ink surround. The palette is parchment `#cfc5a9`, bone `#efe7d3`, dilute grid ink `#6f6757`, reed `#a9ae8a`, silt `#bfa072`, slack water `#5a7680`, cinnabar `#b0402e`, indigo `#3a5f8f`, gilt `#d9a441`, mulberry `#7d5a7e`, and ash violet `#6b5d72`.

The scene owns paint and game marks, not host controls. The host supplies the black stage, rounded clipping, transport, chat, and results. Night ink is intentionally greener than the host chrome. Host mint and sky do not enter the canvas.

Board geometry remains logical and is independent of the viewport. Presentation uses the effective CSS hex radius, the base display scale multiplied by camera zoom: figure at 28 px or more, token from 12 px to below 28 px, and compact below 12 px. The fitted view uses tokens, and deliberate zoom promotes them to figures. Each level preserves unit type through a distinct silhouette, glyph, or shape.

The camera fits every non-void tile into the view at reset. Wheel, drag, and pinch gestures move the battlefield below the screen-fixed HUD, and double-click or double-tap restores the fitted view. Zoom promotes unit art through the same compact, token, and figure thresholds.

Terrain always combines wash and mark. Grassland is a muted bare reed wash with no paired grass tufts. Hills use clearly separated silt contours, water uses broad slack ripples, forest uses canopy, marsh uses one or two sedge tufts, and wasteland uses the ash-violet waste mark. A tile draws its terrain mark first and its feature mark over it. Marks need enough contrast and stroke weight to be understood at token scale.

Units use Sengoku visual language. Footmen are ashigaru with a jingasa, yari, and restrained tate shield. Archers kneel with asymmetric yumi at full draw. Cavalry are mounted samurai with kabuto-shaped horse detail. Token glyphs use bold mon-like masses: a curved katana, an asymmetric yumi, a warhorse head, and a waraji footprint, each with few interior details. Each unit type uses one texture with a heavier white stroke and the original-width black contour. Every tintable runtime asset is a white grayscale-alpha PNG mask that Pixi can multiply by the selected color. Figure rendering adds a small bone edge light where needed and places its oval base near the figure's feet. `FIGURE_BASE_Y_FACTOR` is the single vertical-position parameter shared by the base, its HP ellipse, and the aligned shadow.

Capture zones use a clearly printed mulberry wash with a heavy pale-orchid union border. The center standard is large enough to remain the zone's visual anchor at figure scale.

The exact high-resolution generated originals live in `source-art/`, including superseded variants. Runtime assets in `assets/` are optimized grayscale-alpha PNG masks at their manifest dimensions.

Motion only describes a change: glides, short lunges, pale-bone arrows, rising damage, ink dissolves, and brief capture blooms. `CRANE_TIMING` owns the absolute event phase windows and settled-frame holds. At scale 1, activation holds for 200 ms, the actor follows every tile in its exact executed path at 200 ms a tile, an attack (melee lunge or ranged arc) runs 400 ms from the moment movement ends, and a 700 ms reaction starts 100 ms into the attack when there is a target, or the instant movement ends for a capture-only reaction. Attack and reaction overlap by design, so the ranged arc is still fading as the damage numeral and the death wisp appear. A paced host's cadence scales the event schedule, and the event lasts as long as its latest window needs, so a four-tile charge into a kill visibly takes longer than a step-and-stab. After a visible event settles, the final frame holds for 300 ms when it was ordered by a player controlled at this screen and 200 ms for every other watched event. Snap and invisible updates install directly without a hold. Human mode uses the longer hold for the viewer's own orders and the watched-event hold for companion and opponent events. Spectate and replay use the watched-event hold. Damage numerals use an opaque black outline that stays two CSS pixels wide across display scales. The event actor keeps its gilt seal and movement range through activation and movement, then the acting range clears the moment the attack or a capture reaction starts. An inspected unit's bone range stays visible throughout another unit's event. A defeated target stays intact until the reaction begins. Seeking, mounting, resizing, and repeated ticks show the completed frame directly. Event motion is the replay's content, so the OS reduced-motion preference does not still it; remote desktop sessions report that preference unconditionally. The schedule itself lives in `timeline.ts`, the presentation thresholds and terrain marks in `presentation.ts`, and the palette in `scene.ts`.

## HUD

The canvas keeps the battlefield clear between y 90 and y 746. A quiet top strip holds the small Lato `ROUND` label, its bone mono number, and optional cinnabar and indigo capture seals with their scores. The lower strip holds each side's live footman, archer, and cavalry figures with mono counts. Each corner group sits on a rounded rectangular field of translucent night ink, using the exact scene backdrop color so the fixed HUD stays legible while the board pans and zooms below it. The strip carries no control, caption, or activation text.

Inspection is parchment, not chrome. A board unit or roster pair opens a dilute-ink card with its name and icon-led hit points, movement, damage, range, and vision. A board-unit card also shows its terrain and feature when the terrain parameter is enabled. Skill rows use a skill mark with the literal `shield_wall` or `charge` code when unit abilities are enabled. Board inspection adds a bone ring and bone range wash. This inspected range owns the layer through every event phase, so another unit's activation cannot replace it. The acting unit keeps its gilt range wash only when no board unit is inspected.

At match end the canvas keeps its normal top strip and bottom rosters. The host owns the game-over result and presents it outside the renderer.

## Human play

Under a person's control the picture narrows to what their side perceives. Ground outside that vision takes a night-ink glaze at alpha 0.45 through the tile's own wash-hex mask, so its edges stay soft and the boundary needs no outline; glazed terrain stays identifiable, because the generated battlefield is standing knowledge. Units outside vision are absent rather than ghosted, an actor the perspective cannot see gets no seal and no range wash, and an activation resolved out of sight installs without animating. A perspective switch cross-dissolves the glaze over 200 ms, and reduced motion snaps it. Both roster strips go while fog is on, since a living-unit count is knowledge no unit has, and the strip they occupied carries the order controls instead.

Composing an order uses the gilt vocabulary. Every legal continuation hex takes a gilt wash at alpha 0.25 with a small ink dot; the chosen tiles carry a wet-ink gilt stroke with mono step numbers baked at host scale times camera zoom, so they remain sharp while zooming, and the projected final tile shows the unit ghosted at alpha 0.5. No movement pips appear beside the activated unit. During open human composition only, the activation seal fades from full opacity to 0.35 and back over 1.6 seconds. Reduced motion keeps it steady, as do spectate, replay, and event seals. Composition marks draw above the hover washes and are never suppressed by them, while their hit areas sit below the units so a unit stays hoverable. The offered continuations are the unit's movement range while it is being ordered, so no range wash is drawn under them and hovering it opens its card alone. The centralized `CRANE_TIMING` hold applies after every visible event: 300 ms for the viewer's own order and 200 ms for a companion or opponent event. Spectate and replay use the 200 ms watched-event hold, while snap and invisible updates do not hold. The view then brings the unit acting next to its centre. A unit the perspective cannot see is never followed, and at the fitted zoom the whole board is already on screen, so nothing moves there. The automatic-strike preview is an informational ink thread from the projected final tile, at the ranged arc's weight and dashed on a fixed 6-and-4 rhythm so a strike at an adjacent hex reads the same as a shot across the field. It swells on a 1.6 s period and snaps to its final highlight under reduced motion; candidates tied for nearest each get their own thread, so uncertainty needs no caption. A centered two-button pair occupies the lower strip: Reset on the left and Confirm on the right. Reset uses `glyph-reset.png`, is always drawn but muted and inactive with an empty path, and has accessible name and tooltip `Reset movement`. After at least one selected step, it clears only the selected path, restores the full movement allowance, and recomputes the automatic-strike preview from the origin without submitting an action or restarting the move clock. Endpoint click still undoes one step and activated-unit click still clears the path. Confirm uses `glyph-move.png`, has accessible name and tooltip `Confirm order`, and alone carries the full gilt move-clock perimeter, draining clockwise from the top and turning pale ember inside the last ten seconds. There is no separate countdown.
