## Public: Leaderboards on the environment page

Per [frontend.md](../../docs/specs/frontend.md), the environment page shows the two leaderboards for the current season stacked in one full-width column, with links to historical seasons.

- **Both boards in one column** for the environment's current **released** season. The automated board shows rank, agent, mean normalized score with population spread, weighted mean agent compute time with its matching acted-tick-weighted spread as a separate column, a failure indicator, and a "Replay" link per row to the Stage 4 replay viewer. The human-feedback board shows rank, agent, mean rating with population spread, and count; agents with under three ratings appear unranked. The two boards never merge into one number, mirroring the spec.
- **Separate public targets.** The environment page labels the open submission season and links it to the signed-in user's My Submissions page, where the submit form lives. It separately labels the current play-open season and links an eligible human player into the play dialog. The two targets may differ, and either may still be unreleased. If submissions are open for an unreleased next round, the form is available without exposing its boards. If no season is play-open, the watch/play controls are disabled, but released history stays readable.
- **Cross-game season index.** `GET /api/seasons` lists every season whose submission window is open, play window is open, or results are released, optionally filtered by environment. Its public DTO contains the season id, environment id, label, the three public gates, timestamps, the active non-superseded submission count, and the ended attributed session count. `SeasonsPage.vue` renders one card per season rather than one card per environment, orders open seasons before released history, highlights the submission-open and play-open gate links with the accent badge treatment, shows the environment thumbnail, and routes the card by release, submission, then play priority.
- **History.** Ordinary users see historical released seasons, and selecting one shows that season's boards read-only. Operators see every season in the same table, including fully private unreleased seasons, and may open an unreleased row as an operator preview backed by the admin detail read. A dedicated per-environment, per-season **Leaderboards page/route** (the spec's "Leaderboards" page) hosts the full-width stacked view. It is linkable by URL with the season id, so a released season's boards are shareable. The environment page embeds the current season's boards and links into this page for history.
- **Agent profile placements.** The agent profile page (Stage 5.6) replaces its Stage 6 placeholder with real **leaderboard placements**. For each season the agent competed in, it shows the agent's rank, mean score, and mean agent compute time, read from the public placements route (step 3/5). Each placement links to that season's Leaderboards view.
- **Board-row replays.** Each automated-board row deep-links the representative recording (step 5) through the existing replay route. This makes the spec's "replays linked from board rows" work against the Stage 4 viewer, with no new player.
- Public board reads hit the released-only routes from step 3. Operators may use the restricted season listing and admin detail read from this page to preview an unreleased season, while ordinary users still cannot list fully private seasons or read any unreleased board.

This adds a new Leaderboards route to `frontend/src/main.ts` (e.g. `/environments/:envId/leaderboards/:seasonId?`, defaulting to the current released season). The renderer/registry imports are unchanged.

## Student submission summaries

`GET /api/my/agents` is an authenticated read for the signed-in user's own submission summary. It derives identity only through `requireUser`, takes no owner id, and remains readable to pending users. Its environment rows contain `env_id`, a nullable current-Season summary, and at most three previous-Season summaries. Each Season includes id, nullable label, creation time, release state, its exact non-superseded submission summary (`id`, status, and submission time) or `null`, and nullable mean score.

The response uses batched reads for the user's submissions, relevant Seasons, and a `listPlacementsByUser(userId)` storage query. Previous Seasons follow canonical storage ordering, newest `created_at` first with the existing row-order tie-break, and the current submission-open Season is removed before the three-row limit. The visible attempt is the non-superseded row in that Season. Score matching considers every user attempt in the Season, so a later resubmission does not hide a score earned by an earlier attempt. A score is exposed only while its Season is released.

`MyAgentsPage.vue` includes an environment when it has an open submission Season or user submission history. It renders one flat list of compact rows with the current Season first, marked by an accent stripe and a visually hidden **Current season** label, followed by the three historical summaries with submission-status stripes. Submitted rows show their status and date, while a missing current attempt shows **Not submitted**. The current row renders a result only when released. Previous released placements render **Score N**, including zero and negative values; released Seasons without one render **No score**; unreleased Seasons render **Results not released**. Every Season row is one interactive `UiCard` and descriptive `RouterLink` to `/environments/:envId/agents/:ownerId?season=:seasonId`, with no separate profile link.

The owner-only behavior on `AgentProfilePage.vue` is maintained in [Stage 5.6](../stage-05/6-watch-run-and-agent-profile.md). Its current-Season summary, form refresh events, and reactive query targeting are the receiving half of these My Agents links.

## Operator: admin console

A new operator-only console drives the step-3 admin API. The UI gates it by the `me` answer. The app already fetches `GET /api/me` once via `me.ts`; extend it to report `is_operator` from the step-1 predicate. The console route and its nav entry then render only for operators. The backend gate (step 3) is the real authority; the UI gate just avoids showing dead controls.

- **Season management.** List the environment's seasons, including unreleased ones, declare a new one, and edit its config. The config covers the **match design** (add match configurations and set each one's slot composition of `builtin-naive`/`submission` seats, seeds, and game count), the `deps_version` (now part of the config, defaulted to the current release), and the override blocks (timeouts active; messaging/LLM fields present but labeled "applies in Stage 8/9"). The match editor will not save a match with zero slots, and it surfaces environment slot-count errors from the API. Two edits prompt a confirmation dialog: editing config when runs exist, and changing `deps_version` when submissions exist. The dialog spells out what will be deleted: the existing runs and boards, plus the submissions on a deps change: and sends the step-3 `force` flag. Without confirmation the edit is refused. Declaring creates an `unreleased`, submission-`closed`, play-`closed` season, so the console should make clear to the operator that opening submissions, opening public play, and releasing results are separate actions. This builds on the Stage 4.5 form primitives (`UiField`/`UiInput`/`UiButton`, status badges, dialogs, and existing layout patterns).
- **Lifecycle controls.** Open/close submissions, open/close public play, and release/unrelease are each a clear button reflecting the three independent gates. The current state is shown alongside: a released/unreleased badge, a submissions open/closed badge, and a play open/closed badge. Opening submissions handles `409 open_season_exists` with a direct message that another season is already accepting submissions. Opening play handles `409 open_play_season_exists`, so the operator sees they must close play on another season first. Releasing is the action that exposes the boards on the environment page. Opening play is a separate, independent action: it allows public watch/play and rating writes whether or not the season is released.
- **Season rating prompt.** A small, always-editable text field holds the operator's season-wide rating prompt (the step-3 rating-prompt route). It is separate from the match-design config editor because it stays editable after runs and release. The console makes clear that this prompt is shown to human raters for every agent. It is distinct from each author's own per-agent prompt, which is set on the agent profile (step 6).
- **Run controls and the run-details page.** The console's run-actions row (above Run Configuration) triggers and re-runs the workflow, cancels an in-flight run, links to the leaderboard, and shows the latest run's status. Triggering navigates to a dedicated **run-details page**, which is the single owner of the live stream: while a run is in progress it subscribes to the step-3 WebSocket log-stream endpoint (reusing the Stage 3 socket conventions where practical), rendering the per-match container lines as a log table and the per-game status — with each game's replay link — as a games table, then reloads on the terminal event. Opening a run mid-run renders current progress from the persisted per-game statuses, then tails live log lines; opening an already-terminal run renders the persisted snapshot with no socket. The buffered backlog-on-attach is deferred (step 3/4), so earlier lines are not replayed. A `409 run_in_progress` is surfaced as "a run is already in progress." A `409 empty_schedule` points the operator back to the match design. Cancel is offered for an in-flight run. The console lists the season's past runs and links each to its run-details page. After completion the console shows the computed boards. An unreleased season's boards are visible here before release: this is the verify-before-expose flow.
- **Verify-before-release.** The console renders the unreleased season's boards via the admin board read, so the operator inspects the automated and human boards privately, then releases. A re-run recomputes them in place. Release/unrelease controls whether the environment page sees them.

This adds new admin route(s) to `frontend/src/main.ts` (e.g. `/environments/:envId/admin`), rendered only when `me.is_operator` is true. New typed client wrappers live in `frontend/src/api/client.ts`, following the existing wrapper style: for `/api/me`'s `is_operator`, the public board/history/placements endpoints, the rating endpoints, and the admin endpoints.

## Done when

The Seasons page lists every public season without exposing unreleased configuration or boards. Each card shows active lifecycle actions, its environment thumbnail, and aggregate activity metadata; its primary destination follows released Leaderboards, My Submissions, then Play priority. The environment page shows the current released season's two boards stacked in one full-width column, with per-row replays, links to historical seasons, and separate submit and play targets. The Leaderboards page is URL-linkable per season, lists only released history for ordinary users, and gives operators an all-season history with clearly labelled unreleased previews. Agent profiles show real placements and make the current submission-open Season's submission state explicit. My Agents shows that current Season and up to three prior submitted Seasons per environment as compact rows in one flat list, including released scores, with each row linking directly to its My Submissions target. The contextual tabs read Overview, Leaderboards, My Submissions, and operator-only Manage. The operator console lets an operator drive the full competition cycle the stage's "Done when" describes. The operator can declare and configure a submission-closed, play-closed, unreleased season's match design, set the season's always-editable rating prompt, open/close submissions under the one-open-submission invariant, release/unrelease, and open/close public play under the one-play-open invariant. They can also trigger and re-run the workflow, watch its games and container logs stream live over WebSocket on the run-details page, inspect the unreleased boards, and release. Non-operators see none of the operator-only listing or preview behavior.
