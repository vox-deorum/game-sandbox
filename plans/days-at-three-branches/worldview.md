# Worldview: Crane Reach

This is the setting guide for contributors and students designing Days at Three Branches or its tactical companion. It supplies shared tone and boundaries, not a required plot.

## The world in brief

Crane Reach is a remote district of reed flats, terraced hills, raised roads, and aging magic works. The Court rules on paper but cannot reliably keep peace. Local powers gather soldiers under banners, claim the Court's authority, and fight short wars over roads, harvests, towers, and working magic.

Three Branches is a useful but unsecured village where a river divides into three channels and an old road crosses them. Merchants, travelers, and rumors pass through. Ordinary life continues while skirmishes move across the surrounding countryside.

This is a hard but livable world. People repair tools, tend fields, trade, gossip, make plans, and joke about trouble. War is common, not all-consuming.

## The Court, banners, and temples

The Court remains the accepted center of the realm. Its seal appears on coins, laws, appointments, and land claims, and people use its roads and measures. Few expect its soldiers when trouble begins.

Lords, towns, military companies, and temples organize under banners or keep independent authority. They cite old offices, disputed inheritances, temple charters, and conflicting Court orders. A lord can protect a road or impose ruinous tolls. A temple can support a claim, close a warded crossing, or bargain with a rival.

Large temples hold fields, workshops, towers, and rights to important magic sites. They exchange repair, storage, mediation, and protection for harvest or profit. They seek peace when it protects their holdings and field guards when bargaining fails.

Wars stay small. Soldiers are scarce, fields need workers, and intact infrastructure is valuable. Banners raid, patrol, threaten, and skirmish over crossings, supply stores, and magic sites, then withdraw before a local victory becomes a costly war.

## Magic and work

Magic is a powerful force found in stones, waters, storms, and living things. Most people use expensive, unreliable works such as forever lamps, talkative toys, and automated brooms without understanding them. Craftworkers replace cracked cores and trace broken lines.

Wizards can directly sense, shape, or command magic. They are rare, valuable, and usually attached to a temple, lord, military company, or wealthy town. They raise wards, restore beacons, quiet dangerous sites, and direct battle magic.

Temples train wizards, preserve methods, and control some strong works. A major temple may be a shrine, workshop, granary, refuge, landlord, trader, and stronghold. No single temple speaks for all others.

Old works outlast their makers and purposes. Communities reuse what functions: a beacon tower becomes a watch post, while a damaged ward can protect one side of a field and endanger the other.

## Crane Reach

Crane Reach follows a broad river through wet lowlands and dry terraced uplands. White cranes, tall reeds, red pines, stone bells, lantern posts, and roofed roadside shrines define its look. Raised roads create routes, crossings, and choke points.

The Reach once relied on magic beacons and warded towers. Enough remains to make it valuable, and too little remains to stabilize it. A pump, bridge ward, or beacon can support several villages and become a cause of conflict.

Places should be visually distinct and immediately legible. Bells suggest communication; lanterns suggest safety or stored magic; bridges, gates, wells, and towers explain gathering and conflict.

## Three Branches

Three Branches is the RPG setting. Its [layout and stable features](village.md#districts) are fixed by the village specification.

The village has no fixed mood or story. It may feel prosperous, anxious, welcoming, secretive, neglected, or ready to flee. Its people may support a banner, resent all banners, or avoid politics. Students author NPC roles, personalities, relationships, and rumors.

The visitor has no prescribed origin, profession, loyalty, or purpose.

## The two games

The RPG game shows ordinary life in Three Branches. Routines, conversations, memories, and rumors reveal the wider conflict without a scripted plot. Villagers can discuss a closed road, passing patrol, temple demand, failed ward, or a beacon changing hands.

The tactical game shows clashes elsewhere in Crane Reach. Armies are detachments serving banners, temples, or alliances. Battlefields include roads, terraces, river crossings, beacon sites, and towers. Capture points remain mechanical goals.

Local communication has the same character in both games: shouts, runners, bells, lantern signals, and simple magic beacons. Village communication carries warnings and rumors; battle communication carries target calls, movement signals, and requests for help.

## Naming guide

Use familiar words. Place names combine landscape, color, material, weather, plant, animal, or built-object terms. Ash Steps, Stone Bell, Lantern Field, Red Ford, and Pine Gate fit the setting. Cranes, reeds, terraces, bells, lanterns, pines, and shrines give an eastern character without borrowed-language terms.

Keep future lore minimal. Do not add a detailed cosmology, dynasty list, fixed temple doctrine, ancient timeline, fixed faction roster, or canonical plot unless a game feature requires it.
